"Fly on a Tuesday" is the most repeated piece of advice in cheap travel. We hold our own store of fares from
Bratislava, Vienna, Budapest, Košice and Prague, so we can check it rather than repeat it.

On our data, the Tuesday rule does not hold.

# What we measured

We took every one-way fare we had seen in the previous two weeks from the five home airports to our 12 cities, found
the cheapest fare for each weekday on each route, and kept only the routes where we had a price for all seven
weekdays. That left 50 routes. Then we asked, for each route, which weekday was cheapest.

| Cheapest weekday | On how many of the 50 routes |
|---|---|
| Saturday | 14 |
| Friday | 13 |
| Thursday | 11 |
| Wednesday | 8 |
| Monday | 7 |
| Tuesday | 2 |
| Sunday | 2 |

Measured on 24 November 2026.

Thursday to Saturday took 38 of the 50 routes between them. Tuesday won on two.

# How much the day is worth

The ranking is less useful than the size of the gap, so here it is. Across those same 50 routes:

- the cheapest weekday averaged **€29.60** one way
- the dearest weekday averaged **€54.80** one way
- the middle route had a **€20** gap between its cheapest and its dearest day
- on average, the cheapest day was **44% cheaper** than the dearest

Shifting a departure by a day or two is worth roughly as much as the whole rest of the search put together. That is
the part of the folk wisdom that survives: the day matters a great deal. Which day it is does not transfer from one
route to the next.

# Why you should not take this too far

Four honest limits on the numbers above:

- **Fifty routes is a small sample, and it is one snapshot.** It describes what our store held on one day in November
  2026. It is not a law of the market, and we would expect it to look different in summer.
- **These are one-way fares only.** A return fare is a single price belonging to a pair of days, so it cannot tell you
  anything about a single weekday, and we left all return fares out.
- **Part of the gap is the date, not the day.** A route's cheapest Saturday and its dearest Tuesday may sit in
  different months, and months differ in price. So the 44% is the spread you can actually find across weekdays, not a
  pure day-of-the-week effect.
- **Your route is the only one that matters.** The whole point of the table is that the answer is per route.

Each of our [route pages](/flights) works out the cheapest day of the week for that one route from its own stored
fares, and shows a price calendar per month. That is the number to use, not the table above.

# The month matters more than the day

The same data by month of departure, taking the cheapest return fare on each route to our 12 cities:

| Departure month | Median cheapest return |
|---|---|
| November 2026 | €148 |
| December 2026 | €130 |
| January 2027 | €104 |
| February 2027 | €97 |

The nearer month is the dearest, which is the ordinary pattern: by late November, November departures are
last-minute. The lowest single fare we held was in December, at €26 return, so cheap individual dates exist in every
month. The median is what you should expect; the minimum is what you have to go looking for.

# What to do with all this

1. **Be flexible about the day before you are flexible about anything else.** Use **Whole month** in the
   [search](/search) and read the calendar.
2. **Check your own route rather than a rule.** The cheapest weekday from Vienna to Barcelona has nothing to do with
   the cheapest weekday from Košice to Rome.
3. **Book further out for a predictable price**, and watch the near month only if you can leave at short notice.
4. **Remember what the flight is next to the trip.** Three nights in Rome cost about €63 a day on top of the fare, so
   a €20 saving on the day of departure is real but small. See
   [what a city break actually costs](/guides/what-a-city-break-costs).

Every price here is a fare we have seen and stored, and each one is shown with its own age. Fares move, so check the
real price on the booking site before you decide.
