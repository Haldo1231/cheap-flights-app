Most flight searches start with a question you cannot answer yet: which airport are you flying from? If you live
anywhere between Bratislava and Košice, that question has at least five answers, and the cheapest one changes from
week to week and from city to city.

This site is built so you do not have to answer it first. Every search covers Bratislava (BTS), Vienna (VIE),
Budapest (BUD), Košice (KSC) and Prague (PRG) at once, and the result tells you which airport is cheapest for the trip
you actually want.

# The five are not equal, and that is the point

The five airports differ enormously in how much they fly. One honest way to show that is to count how many
destinations we currently hold a recent cached fare for. This is a measure of our own stored prices, not an airport's
route map, but the ranking matches what you would expect:

| Airport | Destinations with a price in our store |
|---|---|
| Vienna (VIE) | 679 |
| Prague (PRG) | 613 |
| Budapest (BUD) | 597 |
| Bratislava (BTS) | 388 |
| Košice (KSC) | 133 |

Counted on 24 November 2026, over fares seen in the previous seven days.

So Vienna, Prague and Budapest are the big networks, Bratislava is a solid low-cost base, and Košice is thin. If you
live in Košice and search only Košice, you will often conclude that a trip is impossible when it is merely expensive
from your nearest airport.

# When the extra travel pays for itself

The trade is always the same: a bigger airport gives you more flights and often a lower fare, but you have to get
there and back, and that costs money, hours and a little risk.

A few rules of thumb that hold up well:

- **Bratislava and Vienna are effectively one city for this purpose.** Vienna Airport has a direct coach to
  Bratislava that takes roughly an hour, and Slovak Lines, RegioJet and FlixBus all run it. If you are in Bratislava,
  treat Vienna as a second terminal of your own airport and always compare the two.
- **Budapest and Prague are worth it for a bigger saving, not a small one.** These are a few hours away by coach or
  train. A €15 difference is not worth a day of travel; a €90 difference on a family's tickets usually is.
- **Košice is worth searching first anyway.** When a route does exist from Košice, it beats everything else, because
  you skip the ground travel entirely.
- **Count the ground travel in both directions**, and count the night you may have to spend near the airport for an
  early flight. That hidden night is what most often turns a "cheap" foreign departure into the dearer option.

# Flying out of one airport and back to another

There is a trick the big search engines rarely offer: leave from one airport and come back to a different one. Two
one-way tickets, out from Vienna and back to Bratislava, are sometimes cheaper than any round trip from either.

On this site that is the switch **"Fly back to a different airport"**. It is off by default, because it makes the
result harder to read, and it only applies to return trips. When it is on, every price you see is clearly labelled as
either a round trip or two one-way tickets, so you always know what you are buying.

One warning worth repeating: two one-way tickets are two separate contracts. If the first flight is cancelled, the
airline owes you nothing on the second one. Round trips are a little safer for that reason, so the saving should be
real before you choose the split.

# Getting to and from the airports

We deliberately do not print coach and bus fares here. We checked, and the published prices for the same journey
differ between sources, change through the year, and some operators price each departure separately. A number we
wrote down today would be wrong within months, and a wrong number is worse than none.

What is stable: all five airports have a public bus or coach link to their own city centre, and the Vienna Airport to
Bratislava coach runs through the day. Before you commit to a foreign departure, check the operator's own timetable
for the day you would travel, and pay particular attention to the first departure of the morning and the last of the
night. The gap in the small hours is what catches people out.

# How to search all five at once

1. Open the [search](/search) and leave all five home airports selected.
2. Pick your dates, or choose **Whole month** if you are flexible. Flexibility is where most of the saving is.
3. If you have no destination in mind, use **Anywhere**: it ranks every city we hold prices for, from all five
   airports together.
4. Look at the airport code on each result before you get attached to a price.

If you already know the route, the [route pages](/flights) give you one page per route with the cheapest cached fare,
a month-by-month table and a price calendar, and the [deals page](/deals) is a weekly shortlist.

Every price on this site is a fare we have seen and stored, shown with its own age ("price seen 9 hours ago"). Fares
move, so treat them as a guide to where the cheap options are, and confirm the real price on the booking site before
you decide.
